**Ada Wren**: Welcome back. Today I'm talking with Mara about something every engineer says they do, and almost nobody does well: keeping a lab notebook.

> **Ada Wren**：欢迎回来。今天我和 Mara 聊一件每个工程师都说自己在做、却几乎没人做好的事：写实验记录。

**Mara Lin**: I started because my supervisor insisted. It took me about a year to notice that the person who benefited most was me.

> **Mara Lin**：我一开始是因为导师坚持要求才写的。大概过了一年，我才发现最受益的人其实是我自己。

**Ada Wren**: What changed your mind?

> **Ada Wren**：是什么让你改变了想法？

**Mara Lin**: I tried to repeat an experiment from three months earlier and could not. The notes said what I did, but not why I did it.

> **Mara Lin**：我想重做三个月前的一个实验，却做不出来。记录里写了我做了什么，但没写我为什么那样做。

**Ada Wren**: So the reader of the notebook is your future self.

> **Ada Wren**：所以实验记录的读者，其实是将来的自己。

**Mara Lin**: Exactly. I now write four things every time: the date, the goal, the method and the result. Failures get the same four lines.

> **Mara Lin**：没错。我现在每次都写四样：日期、目的、做法和结果。失败的实验也一样写这四行。

**Ada Wren**: And how do you keep it going on busy days?

> **Ada Wren**：那忙的时候怎么坚持下去？

**Mara Lin**: Five minutes before I leave, every day. If I skip that, I've learned the details are gone by the next morning.

> **Mara Lin**：每天下班前五分钟。我发现只要跳过这一步，第二天早上细节就忘光了。
